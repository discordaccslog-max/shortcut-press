An interactive browser guide to motion graphics in Adobe After Effects, built around a ten-second logo animation: compositions and layers, keyframes and transform properties, easing and the graph editor, shape layers and animated text, parenting, precomps and mattes, and timing to music and rendering, with a storyboard, artwork and a music loop.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Projects, compositions and layers

    Set up a project, import files, create a composition with the right size and length, and find your way around the timeline.

  2. 02

    Keyframes and transform properties

    Animate position, scale, rotation and opacity with keyframes, move the anchor point, and read keyframes in the timeline.

  3. 03

    Easing and the graph editor

    Make motion feel natural with Easy Ease, shape speed in the graph editor, and add an overshoot that settles.

  4. 04

    Shape layers and animated text

    Draw shape layers, animate a line drawing on with Trim Paths, add text, and animate it letter by letter with a text animator.

  5. 05

    Parenting, precomps and mattes

    Link layers so they move together, group animation into precomps, and reveal layers through masks and track mattes.

  6. 06

    Timing to music and rendering

    Land key moments on the beat, add motion blur, and render a finished MP4 with the Render Queue or Media Encoder.

Software required

This guide is written for Adobe After Effects (recent Creative Cloud versions) on Windows or Mac. After Effects is not included with the guide; it needs a Creative Cloud subscription or trial, obtained separately from Adobe. Exporting to MP4 uses Adobe Media Encoder, which comes with the subscription, or the H.264 option in After Effects' own Render Queue.

About software updates. Software makers update their programs, so menus and button locations can change between versions. This guide is revised when an update changes the steps, and the revised version appears the next time you open it.

What you need

  • A modern web browser (such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) on a computer, tablet or phone.
  • Adobe After Effects (Creative Cloud subscription), obtained separately, to follow the steps.
  • An internet connection the first time you open the guide on a device; after that it also works offline.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You want to animate logos, titles and graphics for videos or social media.
  • You've opened After Effects and found the timeline and properties confusing.
  • You know a design tool such as Illustrator, Figma or Canva and want things to move.

  • You need visual effects compositing, 3D tracking or rotoscoping in depth.
