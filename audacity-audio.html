An interactive browser guide to editing spoken audio and producing a short podcast in Audacity: navigation and editing, removing noise, hum and clicks, compression and loudness normalization, music beds with fades and automatic ducking, and labels, metadata and export, with a raw test recording full of problems to fix and a music bed.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Projects, waveforms and navigation

    Import audio, read the waveform, play and zoom, and save a project you can come back to.

  2. 02

    Cutting and arranging

    Select precisely, delete and split, move clips, tighten long pauses, and undo freely.

  3. 03

    Removing noise, hum and clicks

    Remove steady hiss with Noise Reduction, cut low hum with a high-pass filter, and repair clicks, in an order that avoids artifacts.

  4. 04

    Levels, compression and loudness

    Avoid clipping, even out loud and quiet parts with compression, and set the overall loudness podcasts expect.

  5. 05

    Music, ducking and fades

    Add a music track, fade it in and out, lower it automatically under the voice with Auto Duck, and adjust volume by hand with the envelope tool.

  6. 06

    Labels, metadata and export

    Mark chapters with labels, export a finished MP3 with metadata, and export sections as separate files.

Software required

This guide is written for Audacity 3 (3.4 or later), the free and open-source audio editor for Windows, Mac and Linux, obtained separately from audacityteam.org; it is not included with the guide. A microphone is useful for recording your own voice but isn't needed: the practice files cover every exercise.

About software updates. Software makers update their programs, so menus and button locations can change between versions. This guide is revised when an update changes the steps, and the revised version appears the next time you open it.

What you need

  • A modern web browser (such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) on a computer, tablet or phone.
  • Audacity 3 (free and open source, for Windows, Mac and Linux), obtained separately, to follow the steps.
  • An internet connection the first time you open the guide on a device; after that it also works offline.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You record a podcast, voice-overs, interviews or lectures and want them to sound clean.
  • You've opened Audacity and weren't sure which effects to use or in what order.
  • You want a simple, repeatable routine from raw recording to finished file.

Who it isn't for

  • You want music production. See Beat Making in LMMS.
