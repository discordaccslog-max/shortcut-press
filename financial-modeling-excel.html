An interactive browser guide to building a clear, checkable financial model in Microsoft Excel for a small café: an inputs sheet, a five-year revenue and cost forecast, a loan schedule with PMT, NPV and IRR, scenario switches, data tables and Goal Seek, and the checks, protection and charts that make a model ready to hand over, with a formula sandbox and practice workbooks.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Model structure

    Separate inputs, calculations and outputs, mark inputs clearly, lay years out across columns, and name the key assumptions.

  2. 02

    The revenue forecast

    Build revenue from drivers (customers, ticket size, days open), then grow it year by year with one formula filled across.

  3. 03

    Costs and operating profit

    Model variable and fixed costs, work out gross and operating profit, and add margin rows that show how healthy each year is.

  4. 04

    A loan schedule

    Work out a loan's payment with PMT, split each payment into interest and principal, and build a schedule that ends at zero.

  5. 05

    NPV and IRR

    Discount future cash flows to today's value with NPV, find the return an investment earns with IRR, and avoid the most common NPV mistake.

  6. 06

    Scenarios and sensitivity

    Switch between low, base and high cases with one input, see how results change with data tables, and work backwards with Goal Seek.

  7. 07

    Checks, charts and handing it over

    Add checks that flag mistakes, protect the formulas, chart the results, and write a short summary so someone else can use the model.

Software required

This guide is written for Microsoft Excel (Microsoft 365, Excel 2021 or Excel 2024) on Windows or Mac. Data tables and Goal Seek are also available in Excel for the web. Excel is not included with the guide; you need your own copy or subscription, obtained separately from Microsoft. The practice workbooks also open in Google Sheets, but the steps follow Excel.

What you need

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You build forecasts or budgets in Excel and want them structured so others can follow them.
  • You're comfortable with formulas and absolute references and want to learn PMT, NPV, IRR and scenario tools.
  • You want to test a business idea's numbers with your own assumptions.

Who it isn't for

  • You want financial, investment or tax advice. The guide teaches modeling techniques with made-up numbers.
  • You need corporate valuation or three-statement models with balance sheets. The guide stays with one small business.
  • You want video lessons, live help, or someone to review your work.
