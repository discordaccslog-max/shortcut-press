An interactive browser guide to making a small 2D platformer in the free Godot Engine 4: projects, scenes and nodes, a player that runs and jumps in GDScript, input actions and tuning, platforms, tiles and a following camera, coins and signals, and a score, sound and an exported game, with sprites, a sound effect and finished scripts.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Projects, scenes and nodes

    Create a project, find your way around the editor, and understand Godot's building blocks: nodes arranged in scenes.

  2. 02

    A player that runs and jumps

    Build a player scene from a CharacterBody2D, a sprite and a collision shape, and write a GDScript that adds gravity, running and jumping.

  3. 03

    Input actions and tuning

    Define your own input actions with keys and controller buttons, and expose speed and jump strength in the Inspector to tune the feel.

  4. 04

    Platforms, tiles and the camera

    Build platforms with static bodies, paint ground with a TileMapLayer, and make the camera follow the player within limits.

  5. 05

    Coins and signals

    Make a coin scene with an Area2D, detect the player with a signal, use groups, and send your own signal when a coin is collected.

  6. 06

    Score, sound and export

    Show a score with a CanvasLayer and Label, play a sound on pickup, restart when the player falls, and export the game.

Software required

This guide is written for Godot Engine 4 (4.3 or later), free and open source for Windows, Mac and Linux, obtained separately from godotengine.org; it is not included with the guide. Use the standard version (not the .NET version); the code is GDScript, Godot's own language, and no prior programming is assumed.

About software updates. Software makers update their programs, so menus and button locations can change between versions. This guide is revised when an update changes the steps, and the revised version appears the next time you open it.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.
