What the guide covers

  1. Navigating the interface

    Moving around the 3D view, selecting objects, switching between object and edit modes, and finding the panels you will use most.

  2. Modeling with basic shapes

    Adding basic shapes, moving, rotating and scaling them, and shaping them further with extrude, inset and loop cuts.

  3. Modifiers

    Working non-destructively with modifiers such as mirror, subdivision surface, bevel and array, stacking them in order, and applying them when you are ready.

  4. Materials

    Assigning materials, setting base color, roughness and metallic values, and giving one object more than one material.

  5. Lighting

    The different light types, a simple three-light setup, and using the world background to light a scene.

  6. Rendering a finished scene

    Placing a camera, choosing render settings, and saving a finished image of the scene you built across the exercises.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, then do the exercises and compare your work with the worked solutions. There is no video, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work. Everything you get is in the .zip.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You have not used Blender before, or opened it once and did not know where to begin.
  • You want to understand the basic modeling, material and lighting workflow from start to finish.
  • You prefer to follow written steps and work at your own speed.

Who it isn't for

  • You want animation, rigging, sculpting, simulation, or game export. Those are not covered.
  • You already model regularly in Blender and want advanced techniques.
  • You want video lessons, live help, or someone to review your work.
