An interactive browser guide to designing app screens in Figma: frames, auto layout, components, styles, and prototyping a simple app screen flow, with downloadable SVG practice assets.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Frames

    Set up frames for different screen sizes, nest frames, and use layout guides to line things up.

  2. 02

    Auto layout

    Build buttons, lists and cards that resize with their content, and control spacing, padding and alignment.

  3. 03

    Components

    Turn repeated elements into components, use instances, change an instance without breaking the link, and organize variants.

  4. 04

    Styles

    Save colors, text settings and effects as styles so a whole design can be updated from one place.

  5. 05

    Prototyping a screen flow

    Link screens together, choose interactions and animations, and preview a simple app flow from start to finish.

Software required

This guide uses Figma. Figma is not included with the guide; you need your own access to it, obtained separately from its maker.

About software updates. Software makers update their programs, so menus and button locations can change between versions. This guide is revised when an update changes the steps, and the revised version appears the next time you open it.

What you need

  • A modern web browser (such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) on a computer, tablet or phone.
  • Figma, obtained separately, to follow the steps.
  • An internet connection the first time you open the guide on a device; after that it also works offline.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You are new to Figma, or have used it only to view designs others made.
  • You want to build screens that are tidy, consistent and easy to change.
  • You want to link screens into a simple clickable flow.

Who it isn't for

  • You want user research methods or design theory in depth. Those are not covered.
  • You want plugin development or turning designs into code. Those are not covered.
  • You want video lessons, live help, or someone to review your work.
