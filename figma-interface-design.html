What the guide covers

  1. Frames

    Setting up frames for different screen sizes, nesting frames, and using layout grids to line things up.

  2. Auto layout

    Building buttons, lists and cards that resize with their content, and controlling spacing, padding and alignment.

  3. Components

    Turning repeated elements into components, using instances, changing an instance without breaking the link, and organizing variants.

  4. Styles

    Saving colors, text settings and effects as styles so a whole design can be updated from one place.

  5. Prototyping a screen flow

    Linking screens together, choosing interactions and transitions, and previewing a simple app flow from start to finish.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, then do the exercises and compare your work with the worked solutions. There is no video, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work. Everything you get is in the .zip.

What's in the .zip

  • The PDF guide

    Each skill explained step by step with annotated illustrations, followed by practice exercises with worked solutions.

  • Starter files

    Figma files that set up each exercise, ready to open in your own copy of Figma.

The software is not included. See Software required below.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You are new to Figma, or have used it only to view designs others made.
  • You want to build screens that are tidy, consistent and easy to change.
  • You want to link screens into a simple clickable flow.

Who it isn't for

  • You want user research methods or design theory in depth. Those are not covered.
  • You want plugin development or turning designs into code. Those are not covered.
  • You want video lessons, live help, or someone to review your work.
