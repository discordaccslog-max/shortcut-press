An interactive browser guide to formatting long documents in Microsoft Word and Google Docs so they stay consistent and easy to update: styles, headings, automatic tables of contents, page layout, and reusable templates, with downloadable Word-compatible practice documents.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Styles

    Apply built-in styles, change a style once so every matching paragraph updates, create your own, and see why formatting by hand causes problems.

  2. 02

    Headings and structure

    Use heading levels to give a document a clear outline, move through it by its headings, and number headings consistently.

  3. 03

    Automatic tables of contents

    Insert a table of contents built from your headings, choose how many levels it shows, and update it after you edit.

  4. 04

    Page layout

    Margins, orientation, page and section breaks, headers and footers, and page numbers that start where you want them to.

  5. 05

    Reusable templates

    Save a finished layout as a template and start new documents from it so every report or letter begins with the same structure.

  6. 06

    Word and Google Docs side by side

    Where the steps differ between the two programs, and how to move documents between them without losing formatting.

Software required

This guide is written for Microsoft Word and Google Docs. You can follow it with either one. Neither program is included with the guide; you need your own access to the one you choose, obtained separately from its maker.

About software updates. Software makers update their programs, so menus and button locations can change between versions. This guide is revised when an update changes the steps, and the revised version appears the next time you open it.

What you need

  • A modern web browser (such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) on a computer, tablet or phone.
  • Microsoft Word or Google Docs, obtained separately, to follow the steps.
  • An internet connection the first time you open the guide on a device; after that it also works offline.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You write reports, handbooks, manuals, or other long documents.
  • You format headings by hand with bold text and font sizes, and spend time fixing them.
  • You want documents that look consistent from the first page to the last.

Who it isn't for

  • You need desktop publishing or print layout. That is not covered.
  • You need mail merge, macros, or tracked changes. Those are not covered.
  • You want video lessons, live help, or someone to review your work.
