An interactive browser guide to building your own monthly budget in Google Sheets: a tidy transactions sheet, totals and category summaries with SUMIF and SUMIFS, a budget-versus-actual table, conditional formatting and charts, and a savings goal tracker, with an in-browser formula sandbox and practice workbooks.

What the guide covers

  1. 01

    Set up the budget sheet

    Import the practice workbook, lay out a transactions table, and format dates and amounts so the rest of the budget is easy to build.

  2. 02

    Totals with SUM and SUMIF

    Add up all income and all expenses, and work out what's left at the end of the month.

  3. 03

    Category summaries

    Build a summary table that totals each category, and use absolute references so one formula fills the whole table.

  4. 04

    Budget versus actual

    Compare what you planned to spend with what you spent, show what's left, and flag categories that went over.

  5. 05

    Conditional formatting and charts

    Make overspending stand out with conditional formatting, and chart where the money went.

  6. 06

    Savings goals and the monthly review

    Track progress towards a savings goal, work out how many months it will take, and set up a routine for the next month.

  • It formats the cells as currency
  • It sorts the list by date

Software required

This guide is written for Google Sheets in a web browser. Google Sheets is not included with the guide; you need your own Google account to use it, obtained separately from Google. The practice workbooks also open in Microsoft Excel, but the steps, menus and shortcuts follow Google Sheets.

About software updates. Software makers update their programs, so menus and button locations can change between versions. This guide is revised when an update changes the steps, and the revised version appears the next time you open it.

What you need

  • A modern web browser (such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) on a computer, tablet or phone.
  • Google Sheets, obtained separately, to follow the steps.
  • An internet connection the first time you open the guide on a device; after that it also works offline.

How the guide works

This is a self-study guide. You read each step, carry it out in your own copy of the software, check your understanding with the quiz, then try the exercise and compare your work with the worked solution. There are no video lessons, no live instruction, no tutoring, and no feedback on your work.

Is this guide a good fit?

Who it's for

  • You want a budget you understand, built yourself rather than copied from a template.
  • You can type into a spreadsheet but haven't used SUMIF, conditional formatting or charts.
  • You'd like a monthly routine for checking where your money goes.

Who it isn't for

  • You want financial, tax or investment advice. The guide teaches spreadsheet skills, not what to do with your money.
  • You need bank connections or automatic imports. The guide works with transactions you enter or paste in.
  • You want video lessons, live help, or someone to review your work.
